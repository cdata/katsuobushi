## What to build

Document how the orchestrator reconciles a branch that no longer applies, and
make the reconciliation visible on the card.

A branch is reviewed against the tip it was based on. It is integrated onto the
tip as it is then. Another card can land in between, so the two are not always
the same, and the reviewer never saw the interaction.

The orchestrator integrates onto the current tip and reconciles what does not
apply. It uses its judgement. In most cases a reconciliation needs no second
review. When a reconciliation changes what the reviewer agreed to, the card
returns to review. That case is rare and is handled one card at a time.

The part that must not be left implicit is the record. A reconciliation is a
change the reviewer did not read, so the card has to say it happened:

- Write what was reconciled into the card's `## Review notes`.
- Say whether the card returned to review, and why.
- Do this before the card moves to `ready`.

Write this into the orchestrator's landing step in
`plugins/katsuobushi/skills/project-orchestration/SKILL.md`, next to the landing
procedure the card moves through.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The skill describes the reconciliation step as part of landing, and says
      the orchestrator uses its judgement
- [ ] A branch that does not apply cleanly leaves a record on the card naming
      what was reconciled
- [ ] The card says whether it returned to review
- [ ] The skill states the test for returning a card to review: the
      reconciliation changed what the reviewer agreed to
- [ ] The record is written before the card reaches `ready`


## Review notes

Peer review by an independent VM: **VERDICT needs-changes**, one blocking item.
Markdown gates clean. The reviewer was given this session's eight landings as
ground truth and tested the rule against them rather than in the abstract.

**BLOCKING — the blanket rule turns the record into noise.** "Every
reconciliation must be written into the card's `## Review notes`" is triggered by
the `BOARD.md` conflict that `git merge --squash` produces on essentially every
landing. Those conflicts are dispatch-seed churn — a guest is seeded from the
orchestrator's working tree, so its branch carries a stale board snapshot the
guest never touched. The reviewer's judgement: a mandatory note about a
mechanical conflict in a file nobody edited "will habituate the product owner to
ignore Review Notes, destroying the signal". The record must be scoped to files
the guest's branch actually changed. One sentence fixes it.

Judged sound: the return-to-review threshold is usable for human judgement, and
the clean auto-merge case (card `7f46b9`, where `sandbox/SKILL.md` merged against
the `deliver` section with no hand-chosen resolution) is correctly excluded by the
"does not apply cleanly" trigger — though only implicitly, so a clarifying
sentence would help. The section satisfies PDD004's test case exactly, including
the negative "it did not return to review, and why not" branch. Placement is
right and does not duplicate the `sandbox` skill's Conflict reconciliation
section — that one is the mechanism, this one is the record — but a
cross-reference to it is missing.
