## What to fix

The whole value of the `needs-review` state is that a second party reaches its
own conclusion. But the orchestrator writes the review directive, and an agent
is highly susceptible to suggestion. Nothing in `project-orchestration` warns
about this, and its own guidance nudges toward it: a good directive is told to
list "what should **block**".

Observed in `cdata/arcanabasis` while driving PDD038. The orchestrator wrote,
in the directive for card `457e37`:

> Recommend BLOCK if you conclude a criterion is unmet in substance. I would
> rather bounce this card than let a criterion quietly go unsatisfied twice.

The reviewer blocked. Its findings were substantiated — it traced a call chain,
proved two match arms were compiler-forced no-ops, read three sibling cards,
and found a mutation that survived 82 passing tests — so the verdict looks
sound. But it is no longer possible to say how much of it was independent
judgement.

The contrast makes the point sharper. Card `580353` had the same class of
defect, a `Capturable:` criterion satisfied by argument rather than by a
capture. Its directive did not invite a block, and its reviewer accepted. Two
reviewers, comparable evidence, opposite verdicts — and the variable that
differed most was the orchestrator's framing.

An accept/block record produced this way reads cleaner than it is.

## What to fix

Add a section to `project-orchestration` on writing a review directive that
does not lead:

- Name what to examine. Do not name the verdict you expect, and do not say
  which way you would rather it went.
- Ask questions rather than asserting findings. "Trace what spawns this
  payload" beats "nothing spawns this payload — confirm".
- Passing on a prior reviewer's finding is legitimate; passing on your own
  conclusion about the current card is not.
- Say plainly that the reviewer may accept, and that an accept is not a failure
  of the review.
- When the orchestrator has a real suspicion, put it in as a question among
  several, not as the centrepiece.

Also worth recording as a known hazard in the "Gotchas learned in practice"
list, next to the existing "reviewer != implementor is a hard rule" note: a
reviewer that is independent in name only is worth less than it appears, and
the orchestrator is the party best placed to compromise it without meaning to.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `project-orchestration` has explicit guidance on not leading the reviewer
- [ ] The guidance distinguishes relaying a prior finding from asserting your own conclusion
- [ ] The existing "what should block" advice is reworded so it does not invite a predetermined verdict
- [ ] A worked example shows a leading directive and a neutral rewrite of it
- [ ] The hazard appears in the gotchas list
